## What this work is

The report asks a narrow workplace-composition question: when personal AI participation, a work-bound organizational system and temporary task executors exchange information or responsibility, which permissions, provenance, role boundaries and resource constraints must still hold at the point of consequential action?

It does **not** claim novelty for multi-agent coordination, federation, role separation, ongoing authorization, provenance, local-first data or cost-sensitive routing. Its proposed contribution is an application-specific requirements synthesis and a matched evaluation agenda for lifecycle transitions such as role change, offboarding, revocation, replacement, correction, delegation, cancellation and recovery.

## Why it matters

A workplace can contain several AI systems that share models or infrastructure while serving different principals. A personal system may retain private history; an enterprise system may retain organizational commitments; a temporary executor may only receive a task-local view. Treating these as one assistant can silently merge permissions, memory and authority that should remain separate.

The report therefore distinguishes:

- authorization from operational readiness and budget;
- memory/role changes from identity or lawful succession;
- transport from permission to use or act on received information;
- task completion from prohibited side effects;
- resource admission from retrospective usage reporting;
- document/test definitions from actual empirical results.

## Evidence and claim boundary

This publication is a conceptual technical report and evaluation design. It contains 38 external and 12 authored source records at explicitly different reading depths. It establishes no integrated implementation, live workplace trial, model-superiority result, economic advantage, consciousness claim, identity-continuity result or c-specific formation effect.

A negative result remains admissible: if a strong conventional implementation already satisfies the proposed obligations with equal or lower burden, the extra profile should be narrowed or merged rather than protected by terminology.
