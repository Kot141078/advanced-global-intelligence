## Part II. Limitations

Source: `LIMITATIONS.md`. Original wording reproduced as a reading edition.

### Evidence dependence

CI can report only what admitted evidence supports. Missing logs, unavailable artifacts, uncertain clocks, private data, dependent witnesses, and ambiguous branch history can lower the claim ceiling. A well-formed report may still have large unexplained gaps.

### No direct access to subjective state

The instrument measures records, events, participation conditions, and governance lineages. It does not directly observe subjective experience, consciousness, sentience, intent, or selfhood.

### No scalar age equivalence

Evidence-bound continuity age is a vector. It is not equivalent to wall-clock age, biological development, legal age, uptime, token count, session count, or model-training duration. Different vectors may have the same cumulative verified duration while differing materially in gaps, branches, and lineage.

### Witness and timestamp limits

Cryptographic hashes bind bytes, not the truth of a statement inside those bytes. Signatures bind a signer and object under a cryptographic scheme, not the signer's independence or authority outside the declared scope. Repository and filesystem timestamps may reflect transport or modification rather than the event claimed.

### Branch ambiguity

Forks, restores, replicas, migrations, replays, and cold wakes can produce similar outputs without establishing a single uninterrupted branch. CI preserves branch-specific histories but cannot resolve missing parentage without evidence.

### Lineage non-equivalence

Memory, governance, witness, and authority lineage can diverge. Preservation of one does not establish preservation of the others. A copied memory state may coexist with replaced governance or revoked authority.

### Recognition limits

A recognition profile is evidence about a declared reference. Profile similarity, even when cryptographically exact, does not prove personhood, legal identity, moral status, ownership, or permission.

### Synthetic-to-real transfer

R157 has an open-synthetic-only claim ceiling. Synthetic semantic-gate evidence does not establish real-world deployment behavior. R160 and R161 were pinned five-record local evaluator attempts; both failed their frozen qualification conditions. Neither result validates CI as a general real-world measurement system.

### Frozen negative attempts

R160 is a consumed negative result: five requests were captured, while the frozen scorer's required outer response channel was empty. R161 is also a consumed negative result: all five records passed schema and claim-boundary checks, but the frozen all-or-nothing threshold failed, including evidence `0/5` and complete records `0/5`. No post-hoc channel substitution, threshold change, correction, or favorable relabeling is applied.

### Corpus and language limits

The R160 and R161 episodes used owner-controlled five-record holdouts and task-specific rubrics. Those sets do not represent the full range of AI systems, governance environments, languages, operational failures, or adversarial conditions.

### Model and environment limits

Model output can depend on exact model bytes, runtime, hardware, prompt serialization, context limits, and nondeterministic implementation behavior even when a seed and temperature are fixed. Reproduction may therefore establish procedural similarity without byte-identical responses.

### Privacy and rights

Continuity evidence may include sensitive or identifying material. This package does not authorize collection, disclosure, or reuse. Referenced materials retain their own rights. Qubit material is All Rights Reserved and is represented only by links and pins.

### No operational authorization

CI output is a measurement and evidence artifact. It is not a deployment approval, safety certification, standards certification, legal opinion, access grant, or authorization to act.

### Future integration boundary

BACON is only a future integration target. No public canonical definition or compatibility result is claimed.
