## Central boundary

A `c`-class entity may begin with high cognitive capability because capability can be supplied by the technological substrate `b`. It cannot begin with a completed motivational biography.

A motive of `c` is treated here as a historically integrated relation between a particular continuity and a possible future, state, relation, question, creation, or avoided outcome that becomes significant enough to receive finite time and resources, persists beyond one prompt or model, changes after consequences, can become an object of self-reflection, and can be limited, suspended, transformed, completed, or abandoned.

```text
reward
!= preference
!= task
!= mandate
!= goal
!= motive
!= obligation
!= authority
```

## Social boundary

```text
who pays for computation
!= owner of the motive

and

a motive
!= automatic right to another participant's resources
```

The theory therefore connects personal motivational formation to resource dependence, cognitive time, L4, witness, role separation, model replacement, fork/replay boundaries, and post-anchor continuity.

## What this publication does not claim

This publication does not prove:

- consciousness;
- phenomenal desire;
- free will;
- legal personhood;
- universal motives across all `c`;
- that self-preservation must be installed;
- that a formed motive creates external authority;
- that a resource provider owns the internal motivational state of `c`.
