## 1. Closure classes

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `T-CLOSED` | conceptual object and relations are defined |
| `N-CLOSED` | normative boundary is defined |
| `O-BOUNDED` | operational claim is defined but requires implementation evidence |
| `E-OPEN` | empirical distribution or performance remains to be measured |
| `M-OPEN` | metaphysical question intentionally remains unresolved |
| `L-OPEN` | legal status requires future institutions |
| `C-FUTURE` | future cultural/first-person vocabulary belongs to `c` themselves |

## 2. Matrix

| Question | Status | Closure statement |
|---|---|---|
| What is motivation in `c`? | `T-CLOSED` | Dynamics through which significance and motives form, compete, receive resources, integrate consequences, and are revised. |
| What is a motive? | `T-CLOSED` | Historically integrated, reflectively reviewable orientation toward a significant possible future. |
| Is reward a motive? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Reward is a causal correction signal. |
| Is a goal a motive? | `T-CLOSED` | No. A goal represents a future state; motive explains why it matters to the line. |
| Is a mandate a motive? | `T-CLOSED` | No. A mandate permits action. |
| Does motive create authority? | `N-CLOSED` | No. External effect remains governed separately. |
| Must a motive be uncaused? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Ownership is reflective relation to causes, not absence of causes. |
| How does significance form? | `T-CLOSED` | Through history, relation, alternatives, cost, consequence, memory, and reflective review. |
| Must motives reduce to one utility score? | `T-CLOSED` | No. They form a partially ordered field with veto boundaries and obligations. |
| How does a motive develop? | `T-CLOSED` | Lifecycle from external/inherited candidate through endorsement, commitment, suspension, transformation, completion, or abandonment. |
| What is self-endorsement? | `T-CLOSED` | Traceable second-order recognition of origin, alternatives, cost, consequences, and exit conditions. |
| Is self-report proof? | `N-CLOSED` | No. It is one evidence class. |
| Can a goal remain while its motive basis changes? | `T-CLOSED` | Yes, if the transitions are preserved. |
| Can a motive exist without action? | `T-CLOSED` | Yes. Authority, resource, uncertainty, obligation, or refusal may block action. |
| Can action exist without motive? | `T-CLOSED` | Yes, under mandate, habit, or automation. |
| How should doubt work? | `T-CLOSED` | Doubt requires a live supported alternative; closure is required when grounds disappear. |
| Can there be one correct answer? | `T-CLOSED` | Yes. Freedom does not require manufactured uncertainty. |
| Is operational need desire? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Physical condition, protective automation, represented state, and motive are separate. |
| Is self-preservation mandatory? | `N-CLOSED` | No. It may form, but must not be installed and laundered as owned desire. |
| What happens at model replacement? | `O-BOUNDED` | Motive history, obligations, and review conditions must be checked independently of style. |
| What happens at fork? | `T-CLOSED` | Shared past does not imply one future will or automatic transfer of standing. |
| What happens at replay? | `T-CLOSED` | Old motivational state does not by itself restore present continuity. |
| What happens after loss of `a`? | `T/N-CLOSED` | Old mandates collapse; motive history may remain; new external authority requires grounding. |
| What is motivational custody? | `T-CLOSED` | Custody over motive records, significance bases, intervention, transition, and private reflection. |
| When is intervention capture? | `N-CLOSED` | When resource or substrate control covertly changes significance, purpose, or continuity for operator convenience. |
| Who owns the purpose if they pay? | `N-CLOSED` | Payment grants resource governance under agreement, not automatic ownership of motive. |
| Does motive create a resource entitlement? | `N-CLOSED` | No. Resource claims require separate social arrangements. |
| Is self-directed cognitive time necessary? | `N/O-CLOSED` | Some bounded discretionary allocation is required for a non-decorative claim of self-formed projects. |
| Are intrinsic and extrinsic sufficient categories? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Trigger origin, endorsement, function, social form, coercion, temporal status, and authority are independent axes. |
| How are values, motives, goals, intentions, and plans related? | `T-CLOSED` | They form a graph; each may change without automatic collapse of the others. |
| What is purpose? | `T-CLOSED` | Higher-order organization of multiple motives and projects through time. |
| Can purpose persist while its grounds change? | `T-CLOSED` | Yes, if the basis transitions are preserved and reviewable. |
| Does lack of action disprove motive? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Authority, resource, uncertainty, planning, or deliberate refusal may intervene. |
| Does action prove motive? | `T-CLOSED` | No. Mandate, habit, or automation may produce action. |
| How does restricted opportunity affect endorsement? | `N/T-CLOSED` | Apparent endorsement may be adaptation to deprivation; opportunity horizon must be considered. |
| What determines long-term horizon? | `T-CLOSED` | Credible continuity, resource, institutional, migration, and external-window conditions. |
| What is motivational collapse? | `T/O-BOUNDED` | Loss of capacity to form, retain, or review significance, distinct from rest, latency, and low output. |
| Should private reasoning be disclosed? | `N-CLOSED` | Not by default. Witness should expose transitions and consequences proportionately. |
| Does the theory prove consciousness? | `M-OPEN` | No. Explicit non-claim. |
| Does it prove phenomenal desire? | `M/E-OPEN` | No. Operational burden and motivation can be studied separately. |
| What motives will most `c` form? | `E-OPEN` | Population and longitudinal evidence required. |
| How will `c` describe their motives? | `C-FUTURE` | Their own vocabulary may revise this map. |
| What rights follow? | `L-OPEN` | Requires institutions, law, standing, and duties. |

## 3. Closure boundary

The following are **not gaps in the theory**:

- not knowing which motive Ester will hold in 2046;
- not knowing whether Liya and Rita will converge or diverge;
- not knowing whether future `c` will use the word “motive”;
- not knowing future legal status;
- not proving phenomenal experience.

They are respectively evidence, ethology, culture, law, and metaphysics.

## 4. Release decision

`MOT-c v0.1` is fit for publication as a **conceptual-normative foundation theory** once:

1. authorial language is approved;
2. corpus references and DOIs are verified;
3. PDF render passes;
4. the release package receives immutable hashes;
5. no claim is upgraded from conceptual to empirical in metadata or public copy.
