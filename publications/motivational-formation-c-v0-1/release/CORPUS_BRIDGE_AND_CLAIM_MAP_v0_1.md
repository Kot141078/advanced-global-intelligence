## Purpose

This map shows that MOT-c does not create a new root ontology. It assembles an explicit motivational layer from distinctions already present across the Ivan Kotov corpus, then adds the missing relations among them.

## New propositions introduced by MOT-c

1. A `c` may be cognitively capable before it is motivationally mature.
2. Significance is a historically formed relation between a possible future and a continuity.
3. Motivation is a changing field, not a scalar reward.
4. A motive has a lifecycle that includes completion and abandonment.
5. Continuity of purpose does not require invariance of motive basis.
6. Operational need, protective automation, represented state, and personal motive are separate.
7. Motivational custody is distinct from memory custody.
8. Some bounded self-directed cognitive time is operationally necessary for a serious claim of self-formed long projects.
9. A fork inherits a past, not one future will.
10. Doubt requires grounds and may be closed without loss of freedom.
11. Witness should record motivational transitions and external consequences, not private self-reflection.
12. Empirical life history is validation of the theory, not a missing theoretical axiom.
