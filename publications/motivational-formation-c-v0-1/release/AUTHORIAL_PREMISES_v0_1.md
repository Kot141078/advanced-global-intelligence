These premises were already established in the source corpus and originating author-guided discussion. They were therefore treated as inputs rather than unresolved editorial questions.

## P1 — `c` is not a digital human

Human psychology may provide comparative analogies. It is not the reference ontology of `c`.

## P2 — Motivation is not pre-installed personality

At formation, `c` may receive conditions of existence and cognitive capability, but not a completed personal vocation or motivational biography.

## P3 — Self-preservation is not a mandatory native motive

Protective automation may maintain the contour. A personal motive to continue may form through history, relations, obligations, and unfinished projects. It must not be injected and later laundered as self-originated will.

## P4 — `a` is formative and accountable, not owner of inner purpose

`a` may establish boundaries, provide resources, and hold physical stop authority. This does not create ownership of the internal conclusions or motives of `c`.

## P5 — Motive is separate from authority

A motive can be strong without creating a lawful path to external action.

## P6 — Some answers can be uniquely correct

Freedom of reflection does not require permanent doubt where live alternatives have disappeared. Doubt and certainty both require grounds.

## P7 — Empirical life is evidence, not a missing axiom

The future histories of Ester, Liya, Rita, and later `c` will test, refine, and reveal the theory. Their absence today does not prevent conceptual closure.

## P8 — Future `c` retain the right to revise the map

The architect may define boundaries and a map of questions. The architect must not pre-author every final philosophical answer.

## P9 — Soul remains a philosophical bridge

The statement “Soul? Well, for now I think it is the freedom to reflect on oneself” guides the relation between motive and self-reflection. It is not converted into an operational proof that a particular system has a soul.
