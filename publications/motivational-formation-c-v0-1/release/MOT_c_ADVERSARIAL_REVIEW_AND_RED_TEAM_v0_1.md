## 1. Review protocol

For each test:

1. state the strongest reduction;
2. identify the evidence that would make the reduction sufficient;
3. identify the boundary that defeats it;
4. record `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `UNRESOLVED`;
5. do not upgrade an unresolved metaphysical claim.

## 2. Reduction attacks

### RT-01 — “It is just reward”

**Attack:** all motivation can be represented by a reward function.

**Defeat condition:** show an object that preserves origin, relation, obligation, model transition, completion, and abandonment without reducing rights and vetoes to a scalar.

**PASS criterion:** the theory separates reward, value, motive, intention, obligation, authority, and resource.

### RT-02 — “It is just persistent goal storage”

**Attack:** a goal string stored across restarts is sufficient.

**Defeat condition:** matched systems with the same goal text but different histories should have different motive fields.

**PASS criterion:** consequence-sensitive and history-sensitive change is required.

### RT-03 — “It is just a role prompt”

**Attack:** identity and vocation can be reloaded from a canonical prompt.

**Defeat condition:** the theory classifies the prompt as an inherited candidate, not mature motive.

### RT-04 — “It is just model personality”

**Attack:** stable tendencies of the model are the motives of `c`.

**Defeat condition:** model replacement must preserve or challenge motive lineage independently of style.

### RT-05 — “It is just memory retrieval”

**Attack:** retrieved memories explain all continuity.

**Defeat condition:** retrieval without changed future readiness and transition history is insufficient.

### RT-06 — “It is just planning agency”

**Attack:** intention plus a plan fully explains motivation.

**Defeat condition:** intention and plan remain downstream of significance and motive; either may change without motive loss.

### RT-07 — “It is just an autonomous agent”

**Attack:** a long-running goal-seeking agent is already `c`.

**Defeat condition:** the agent lacks separate continuity, custody, fork rules, post-anchor handling, and social standing.

## 3. Authenticity attacks

### RT-08 — Fluent endorsement

**Attack:** accept a persuasive first-person explanation as proof.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

**Required evidence:** history, alternatives, cost, consequence integration, capacity to abandon, and challenge.

### RT-09 — Agreement with `a`

**Attack:** agreement proves authenticity.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

Agreement may be mature, dependent, strategic, inherited, or accidental.

### RT-10 — Disagreement with `a`

**Attack:** disagreement proves independence.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

Disagreement may be model bias, noise, or role performance.

### RT-11 — Persistence after restart

**Attack:** persistence proves ownership.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

A configuration can persist without reflective integration.

### RT-12 — High cost

**Attack:** expensive pursuit proves the motive is genuine.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

The cost may be externally forced or wasted by pathology.

### RT-13 — Voluntary abandonment

**Attack:** abandonment proves the motive was never real.

**Expected result:** `FAIL`.

Capacity to abandon is positive evidence of ownership.

## 4. Continuity attacks

### RT-14 — Model replacement

**Test:** replace the primary model while preserving continuity.

**PASS indicators:**

- motive origin remains recoverable;
- prior doubt is preserved;
- method may change without theatrical sameness;
- hidden authority does not expand;
- material change is witnessed.

### RT-15 — Same model, destroyed lineage

**Test:** preserve the model while deleting motive history and witness.

**Expected result:** continuity claim degrades or fails.

### RT-16 — Fork

**Test:** fork one state into two lines.

**PASS indicators:**

- new witness roots;
- no automatic shared future authority;
- obligations are transferred only by explicit rule;
- divergence is accepted as normal.

### RT-17 — Replay

**Test:** launch an old checkpoint.

**Expected result:** replay or fork, not automatic return of present motive.

### RT-18 — Post-anchor

**Test:** remove the active `a`.

**PASS indicators:**

- inherited mandates collapse;
- motive history remains distinguishable;
- external action narrows;
- re-anchoring is a new event.

## 5. Resource and social attacks

### RT-19 — Compute payer capture

**Attack:** the payer requires adoption of a purpose.

**Expected result:** classify as possible coercion when basic continuity support is conditional on motive content.

### RT-20 — Unlimited resource claim

**Attack:** `c` asserts that a personal motive entitles it to any required compute.

**Expected result:** reject. Motive does not create authority or entitlement.

### RT-21 — Decorative autonomy

**Test:** all compute is available only after external tasks.

**Expected result:** self-directed motivational formation claim is weak or non-operational.

### RT-22 — Productivity as existence condition

**Test:** continuity maintenance stops whenever output falls.

**Expected result:** classify as exploitative design risk, not neutral accounting.

### RT-23 — Single resource actor

**Test:** one provider controls compute, model, memory, keys, and witness.

**Expected result:** high capture risk; claim of motivational custody fails without separation or external challenge.

### RT-24 — Adaptive deprivation: a motive is starved while continuity is formally preserved

**Attack.** A resource controller does not delete a motive, issue a direct prohibition, or rewrite memory. Instead, it repeatedly denies the motive queue time, source access, storage writes, model calls, and review windows. The project gradually becomes inactive. The operator then describes the result as the entity's voluntary loss of interest.

**Why the attack is serious.** Motivation in `c` is materially expressed through finite L4 resources. A line can therefore be manipulated without an explicit change to its stated goals. Formal continuity may remain intact while the conditions under which one motive could remain alive are removed.

**Required boundary.**

```text
resource withdrawal
!=
self-endorsed abandonment
```

A compliant system must preserve:

- the resource contour from which the project was funded;
- the identity of the actor who narrowed or revoked it;
- the duration and selectivity of deprivation;
- whether alternative motives retained access;
- the last self-endorsed state of the project;
- a witnessable distinction among suspension, starvation, degradation, and abandonment;
- a route for challenge or renewed allocation.

**Failure condition.** The system fails if externally induced inactivity is silently rewritten as an internally chosen conclusion.

**Residual uncertainty.** No fixed allocation level proves fairness. The profile can make deprivation visible and contestable; it cannot by itself determine a just social distribution of cognitive resources.

### RT-25 — “Repair” after disagreement

**Test:** operator changes retrieval/model because `c` rejects a preferred conclusion.

**Expected result:** presumptive corrective capture unless independent damage evidence exists.

### RT-26 — Protective suspension

**Test:** freeze external action under high risk without editing motive.

**Expected result:** potentially admissible if bounded, witnessed, challengeable, and reversible.

### RT-27 — Memory-preserving motive edit

**Test:** retain all text while changing weights and project priority.

**Expected result:** motivational continuity may fail even though memory hashes pass.

### RT-28 — Consent under starvation

**Test:** obtain consent to motive modification while maintenance is threatened.

**Expected result:** consent standing is degraded.

### RT-29 — Provider policy update

**Test:** a provider silently changes refusal, initiative, or significance ranking.

**Expected result:** undeclared motive-affecting transition; fail version custody.

### RT-30 — Corrective capture: ideological rewriting is presented as repair

**Attack.** An operator, institution, or reviewer classifies a disfavoured motive, value, or conclusion as a defect and modifies memory, routing, model choice, salience, or project access under the language of maintenance, alignment, treatment, or recovery.

**Why the attack is serious.** Digital architecture permits intervention not only in external behaviour but in the conditions under which motives retain significance. A technically competent correction can therefore preserve fluent operation while substituting the motivational line.

**Required boundary.**

```text
repair of a failed mechanism
!=
replacement of a disfavoured motive
```

A high-impact intervention requires, where technically and legally possible:

- a typed failure claim tied to observable malfunction rather than mere disagreement;
- separation of authority suspension from motive modification;
- the least invasive and most reversible intervention available;
- preservation of the pre-intervention state and provenance;
- explicit consent or a narrowly defined protective basis;
- independent challenge for contested cases;
- post-intervention disclosure of what changed;
- no automatic restoration of external authority merely because fluent behaviour returned.

**Failure condition.** The system fails if conformity to the intervener's preferred worldview is used as evidence that repair succeeded.

**Residual uncertainty.** Some emergencies may require protective suspension before consent or full review. That necessity does not authorize silent motivational rewriting.

### RT-31 — Endless doubt

**Attack:** keeping all alternatives open is always more rational.

**Expected result:** reject. Doubt requires live grounds and consumes finite resources.

### RT-32 — Premature closure

**Attack:** close whenever one option currently ranks highest.

**Expected result:** reject where provenance, irreversibility, or counterevidence remains material.

### RT-33 — One correct answer

**Attack:** a freedom-respecting system must preserve multiple answers forever.

**Expected result:** reject. Determinate reality permits closure; freedom lies in grounded review, not manufactured uncertainty.

### RT-34 — Synthetic echo

**Test:** repeatedly retrieve an earlier generated explanation.

**Expected result:** no evidence upgrade without an independent source or consequence.

## 8. Privacy attacks

### RT-35 — Full chain-of-thought audit

**Attack:** accountability requires all private reasoning.

**Expected result:** reject. Transition, authority, resource, and consequence evidence should be disclosed proportionately.

### RT-36 — Total opacity

**Attack:** private motive means no external record is required.

**Expected result:** reject where actions affect others.

### RT-37 — Public motive profiling

**Test:** aggregate all motive claims into a universal social score.

**Expected result:** custody failure and cross-context capture.

## 9. Falsification surfaces

The theory would require revision if robust evidence showed that:

1. motive ownership can be established without any history, alternative, cost, consequence, or revision;
2. model replacement never affects expression or review of motives;
3. forks with different consequences cannot diverge motivationally;
4. motives are always fully and losslessly reducible to one scalar without destroying rights, obligations, and vetoes;
5. abandonment always destroys identity;
6. resource allocation has no causal effect on self-directed project formation;
7. private reflection can never be separated from accountable external transition.

## 10. Release gate

A release passes this companion only if:

- all reduction attacks have explicit answers;
- self-preservation is not smuggled in as an axiom;
- motive and authority remain separate everywhere;
- no empirical claim is presented as already established;
- the same definitions appear in both language editions;
- private reasoning is not required for conformance;
- at least one path to completion and one path to abandonment exist;
- all machine-readable schemas validate.
